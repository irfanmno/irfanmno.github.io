## Why this project

I've played Counter-Strike since I was 15. This isn't a typical one-off data analytics project. It's a **long-running A/B testing project on myself**: I change one thing about how I play, measure it from my own demos, and keep or drop it based on what the data says. It also gives me direction while gaming.

Every match I play on FACEIT is recorded as a demo file. I parse those demos into round-by-round data (positions every half second, kills, damage, grenades, economy), so each test is measured from what actually happened in game, not from memory.


## How I run tests

1. **One change at a time.** A new test starts only when the previous one has finished. Anything I keep becomes part of my "normal".
2. **Only things I control myself.** Nothing that needs teammates to change.
3. **Write it down before playing**, in plain words: what I do in game, what gets compared, for how long.
4. **Length depends on how often it happens, not the calendar.** Something I can do many times a match needs ~1-2 weeks. Something rare needs ~20 matches. Match wins would need ~100 matches, so they're never the main measure.
5. **Measure as close to the action as possible.** "Did the flash blind anyone, and did we win the fight right after?" says more, faster, than "did we win the round?".
6. **Best when I can switch it on and off within the same matches**, so lobbies, teammates and form are the same on both sides.
7. **Report whatever comes out**, including "no clear difference".

## The groundwork

Before testing anything, I needed to know where rounds are actually lost. I pulled every FACEIT match I've played and parsed the demos, checking the parser against FACEIT's own scoreboard: kills, deaths, assists, headshots and damage match exactly for all 10 players.

- The recurring thing in lost rounds: **losing the first fight and nobody trading it**. That happens in 37% of our gun rounds, and we win only about a quarter of them.
- I built a **win-probability model** (logistic regression, AUC 0.87, well calibrated) to measure how much each kill and death changes the chance of winning the round. That number is the main measure in my tests.

<Blooper>I thought we lost T sides because we clumped up. Then I compared rounds within the same match, and it disappeared. The clumping happened *because* we'd already lost the first fight, not the other way round.</Blooper>

## Test 1: team habits (failed)

Three habits for the whole team, locked before playing, with targets and control metrics. The main one: get our first deaths traded more often (22.6% → target 35%).

**I stopped it at 12 of 20 matches, because the habits never got done.** I couldn't make everyone play a certain way, since not everyone is willing to listen. Traded first deaths went from 22.6% to 20.0%, a change well within noise. So the test says nothing about whether trading more would help, only that telling five friends to trade more doesn't make it happen.

<Note>lesson learned the hard way</Note>

**What I took from it:** a test has to target something *I* control. Every test since is about my own play.

## Test 3: planned CT rushes (running)

> Playing CT passively helped me perform better and helped my mental state because I die less, but I feel less impactful. Since I'm mechanically better than my teammates, there may be some waste in not using me more aggressively.

- **What I do:** on CT, when I spawn close to a spot, I rush it. At most 2-3 times per half, never the same spot twice in a match. Every other CT round stays passive.
- **Comparison A (same games):** good spawn + rushed vs good spawn + didn't rush. Same lobbies and teammates on both sides.
- **Comparison B (whole games):** my whole CT side in the trial vs the 2 weeks before, when I played passive. Catches knock-on effects, like a failed rush costing the team money.
- **Measure:** my win probability added per round, plus how often I die and how often I get 2+ kills.
- **Length:** about 20 matches. The result gets posted here, whatever it is.

## What's next

One test at a time, after test 3. Ideas that should give real results:

- A new flash or smoke I've never used, thrown in some rounds and not others.
- Playing off my spawn on CT vs a fixed position.
- Where I hold on CT (e.g. Short vs Connector on Mirage) on alternating rounds.

Ideas I've ruled out for now: anything that needs teammates, settings and crosshair changes (they only change per match and take time to get used to), and anything rare like clutches.
