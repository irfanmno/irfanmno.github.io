## Intro

I've played Counter-Strike since I was 15. This isn't a typical one-off data analytics project. It started with one question, **why do we lose rounds?**, and turned into a **long-running A/B testing project on myself**: I change one thing about how I play, measure it from my own demos, and keep or drop it based on what the data says. It also gives me direction while gaming.

Every match I play on FACEIT is recorded as a demo file. I parse those demos into round-by-round data (positions every half second, kills, damage, grenades, economy), so everything is measured from what actually happened in game, not from memory.

All the code, notebooks and experiment write-ups are on [GitHub](https://github.com/irfanmno/cs2-analysis).

## Exploration

Before changing anything, I needed to know where rounds are actually lost. I pulled all 138 of my FACEIT matches and parsed 64 demos, checking the parser against FACEIT's own scoreboard: kills, deaths, assists, headshots and damage match exactly for all 10 players.

<Blooper>My first damage numbers were way too high. I was counting overkill: a 400-damage AWP shot on a player with 100 HP.</Blooper>

Five notebooks, one question each:

- **First look:** what do match-level stats say (maps, form, who carries)?
- **Why we lose rounds:** what happens before and during the first fight?
- **Enemy utility:** does their utility beat ours?
- **My impact:** which kills and deaths actually change the round?
- **Good vs bad T sides:** what do our good halves do differently, and is it a cause or a consequence?

## Findings

**What mattered:**

- **Losing the first fight untraded.** It happens in 37% of our gun rounds, and we win only about a quarter of them. Getting the first kill wins ~70%.

- **We win when the whole team shows up.**

- **A win-probability model for every moment of a round** (logistic regression on players alive, side, bomb and equipment; AUC 0.87 on unseen matches, well calibrated). It turns each kill and death into "win % added", which became the main measure in my experiments. I add the most on CT (+4.3 per round), but little on T.

<Figure src="/img/cs2/04_wpa_by_player.png" alt="Net win percentage added per round by player, T and CT" caption="impact per round, by side" />

- **One lead:** 2+ mid smokes before the first kill on Mirage T goes with +10 pts on the opening duel. I picked that cut-off after seeing the data, so it still needs a proper test.

**What didn't hold up:**

- Being "disjointed": we trade as often as our opponents do.
- Getting held at site entrances: 22% for us vs 23% for opponents.
- Utility damage: their nades don't out-damage ours.
- Tilt: no sign that a loss makes the next match worse.

<Blooper>I thought we lost T sides because we clumped up. Then I compared rounds within the same match, and it disappeared. The clumping happened *because* we'd already lost the first fight, not the other way round.</Blooper>

## Becoming an A/B testing project

Knowing *why* we lose rounds only helps if I change something and check whether it worked. So from here the project has no end: **one experiment at a time, each followed by its findings, then the next.**

The rules I stick to:

1. **One change at a time**, and only things I control myself.
2. **Written down before playing**: what I do in game, what gets compared, for how long, and what counts as success.
3. **Length depends on how often it happens.** Something I do many times a match needs ~1-2 weeks, something rare ~20 matches. Match wins would need ~100 matches, so they're never the main measure.
4. **Compare within the same matches when possible**, and check a control (what opponents did) so a change in lobbies isn't mistaken for a change in me.
5. **Report whatever comes out**, including "no clear difference".

## Experiment #1: team habits

Straight from the findings: if losing the first fight untraded is the problem, fix that as a team. Three habits, locked before playing, each with a target and a control:

- **Trade the first fight** (main one): get our first deaths traded more often, 22.6% → 35%. Control: how often *opponents'* first deaths get traded.
- **One more nade before the first fight.** Control: opponents' nades in the same matches.
- **Me playing back on CT**, where the model says I add the most.

Planned length: 20 matches. Success = the 95% bootstrap interval (resampling whole matches) is entirely on the right side of zero, and the control didn't move the same way.

## Experiment #1: findings

**I stopped it at 12 of 20 matches, because the habits never got done.** I couldn't make everyone play a certain way, since not everyone is willing to listen.

<Figure src="/img/cs2/exp01_results.png" alt="Change in each metric between baseline and experiment, with 95% intervals" caption="every locked metric, experiment vs baseline, with 95% intervals" />

- **Traded first deaths: 22.6% → 20.0%**, well within noise. CT trades actually got worse.
- **The controls stayed flat**, so it wasn't the lobbies changing.
- So the test says nothing about whether trading more would help, only that telling five friends to trade more doesn't make it happen.

<Note>lesson learned the hard way</Note>

**What I took from it:** an experiment has to target something *I* control. Every experiment since is about my own play.

## Experiment #2: MAC-10 vs AK (withdrawn)

A random card would decide which gun I buy on T rounds where I can afford a rifle. I withdrew it before playing a single match: the gun changes *where* I play (the MAC-10 means close-range spots), so it could never isolate the gun itself.

## Experiment #3: planned CT rushes

> Playing CT passively helped me perform better and helped my mental state because I die less, but I feel less impactful. Since I'm mechanically better than my teammates, there may be some waste in not using me more aggressively.

- **What I do:** on CT, when I spawn close to a spot, I rush it. At most 2-3 times per half, never the same spot twice in a match. Every other CT round stays passive.
- **Comparison A (same games):** good spawn + rushed vs good spawn + didn't rush. Same lobbies and teammates on both sides.
- **Comparison B (whole games):** my whole CT side in the trial vs the 2 weeks before, when I played passive. Catches knock-on effects, like a failed rush costing the team money.
- **Measure:** my win probability added per round, plus how often I die and how often I get 2+ kills.
